# What Gets Wet — R4 Demo Filmmaker + Cast Guide V5

**Working production guide for Anthony Gould, Matt Henderson, Nextspace, and edit support**  
**Target demo runtime:** 15–20 minutes  
**Target combined opening:** Anthony presentation + WGW demo within first 30 minutes  
**Primary edit environment:** Final Cut Pro  
**Delivery format:** Live narration over short, local pre-recorded Nextspace vignettes  
**Core principle:** **The presenters drive the room. The video supports the presenters.**

---

## 1. What we are making

This is not a conventional software demo and it is not one long screen recording.

We are making a set of short, polished **visual vignettes** that Matt and Anthony can call up non-linearly during the live session. Each vignette should feel like a camera move into a different aspect of the same connected world.

The audience should never feel that they are watching a playlist, training video, Nextspace feature tour, digital twin sales demo, or chatbot demonstration.

The audience should feel that they are following an engineering investigation:

> **What might happen?**  
> **What is exposed?**  
> **What depends on it?**  
> **What does the evidence actually support?**  
> **What else do our experts know?**  
> **Can the same context be reused?**  
> **What changes when we carry that context across time, scale, and decisions?**

The digital twin is the visual stage.

**The reusable context is the hero.**

---

## 2. Performance objective

Anthony’s presentation immediately before the demo establishes:

- AI is getting smarter faster than enterprises are making meaning usable.
- Humans have historically compensated for missing context.
- Trusted AI requires four conditions: Curated, Context, Controlled, Connected.
- More context is not the same as shared context.
- Important meaning should not have to be reconstructed every time a new question is asked.
- Shared context becomes reusable infrastructure.

The WGW demo should **make those ideas visible without re-teaching them**.

Do not stop and say, “Here is the ontology.”

Do not stop and say, “Here is the knowledge graph.”

Instead, let the audience see:
- one scenario related to time,
- water related to places,
- places related to buildings,
- buildings related to values and communities,
- facilities related to service areas,
- service areas related to dependent buildings,
- sources related to claims,
- engineering guidance related to decisions,
- and the system explicitly separating what is known from what is inferred.

By the time the audience hears terms like ontology or graph later, they should already understand the behavior intuitively.

## 4. Recording language: think like a filmmaker, not a screen recorder

Every clip should have a beginning, a reveal, and a landing frame.

A good vignette:
1. starts already oriented,
2. performs one meaningful move,
3. reveals one new relationship or consequence,
4. ends on a frame worth discussing for 20–60 seconds.

Avoid:
- long mouse travel,
- spinning loaders,
- menu hunting,
- dead cursor time,
- repeated panel opening,
- browser tabs,
- unnecessary typing,
- accidental zooms,
- “let me show you where this is” navigation,
- recordings that end while the interface is still moving.

Prefer:
- purposeful cursor movement,
- one action per beat,
- slow enough motion to read,
- decisive framing,
- clean final holds,
- hard cuts hidden by natural camera changes,
- speed ramps only when needed to compress dead navigation.

---

## 5. Capture standard

Record all Nextspace material at:
- **1920×1080**
- **30 fps**
- same browser zoom
- same Nextspace viewport dimensions
- same OS scaling
- same position of native Nextspace controls
- same top-left 9-dot launcher location

Record without live spoken narration. The live presenters carry the performance.

### Cursor

The cursor is part of the performance. Use it to direct attention, not merely to click.

When the audience should look at a scenario control, service-area polygon, source citation, Ask result, building, or asset, let the cursor arrive and **hold briefly** before the next action.

### Final frame

Every vignette must end on a stable frame.

Do not fade to black.

The last frame is a stage backdrop.

# SCENE 02 — MODEL THE EVENT

**Launcher title:** **What might happen?**  
**Clip target:** 45–70 sec  
**Live chapter target:** ~3:00

This scene is intentionally longer than earlier versions.

### Editorial objective

Teach the physical model **without using architecture vocabulary**.

The audience should intuitively understand:
- this is a scenario,
- scenarios differ,
- the event changes through time,
- water intersects real places,
- exposure can be quantified,
- modeled intersection is not the same as damage.

### Shot A — regional model

Start with the hydro model visible.

Give the audience 2–3 seconds to orient.

Matt:

> “This is the hydrodynamic model — our representation of how water moves through this system under a particular scenario.”

### Shot B — time progression

Move cleanly through:
- Start
- Rising
- Peak
- Recession

Matt:

> “Instead of only asking whether a location ever gets wet, we can watch how that exposure changes through the event.”

### Shot C — scenario comparison

Move once between:
- Sandy
- 100-year
- 100-year + sea-level rise

Matt:

> “We can compare different scenarios without changing the underlying geography or assets.”

### Shot D — exposure plugin

Reveal:
- assessed/appraised property value exposed,
- buildings exposed,
- population / housing exposure,
- vulnerability indicators.

Matt:

> “Now we can move from the water itself to what the water intersects.
>
> These are screening-level exposure measures.
>
> Property value here means assessed or appraised value intersecting the modeled exposure — not modeled damage or loss.
>
> Buildings exposed means the model reaches the building footprint — not that we have proven interior flooding.
>
> And the population and community measures help us see where exposure may have broader human consequences.”

### Anthony — optional

> “Without calling it anything technical, we are already relating a scenario, a time, a place, buildings, value, and communities.”

### End frame

End with a readable scenario, exposure metrics visible, and strong geography.

Hold.

### Final Cut notes

Build from several shorter source recordings if needed. Remove wait states between toggles. Hard cuts between scenario states are fine if visually clean.

# SCENE 03 — BRANFORD: FROM EXPOSURE TO DEPENDENCY

**Launcher title:** **What else is affected?**  
**Clip target:** 35–50 sec  
**Live chapter target:** ~2:00

### Critical terminology before recording

Rename the entity:

**Branford Water Pollution Control Facility (WPCF)**

Do not call this:
- Branford Water Treatment Plant,
- Branford Water District,
- potable water service.

The CT DEEP layer is **Connected Sewer Service Areas**. It represents areas connected to sanitary sewers and identifies treatment facilities where wastewater is processed.

### Editorial objective

Move from:

> **Where is the exposure?**

to:

> **What depends on the exposed system?**

### Shot A — approach Branford

Begin from the exposure map and move toward Branford.

### Shot B — reveal WPCF

Turn on or focus the WPCF. Let it sit for a beat.

### Shot C — reveal sewer service area

Turn on the CT DEEP Connected Sewer Service Area.

### Shot D — reveal dependent buildings

Reveal buildings inside / linked to the service geography.

Tune polygon opacity so the service area is obvious but the facility and buildings remain legible.

### Matt

> “The hydro model tells us where the modeled water is.
>
> But the more consequential question is what those exposed things do — and what else depends on them.
>
> This is Branford’s Water Pollution Control Facility.
>
> CT DEEP identifies the connected sewer service area associated with treatment endpoints like this one.
>
> That gives us a path from the facility, to the service geography, to the buildings that depend on that wastewater system.”

Pause.

> “Exposure does **not** mean the plant is offline.
>
> But if continuity here were compromised, the consequence could extend far beyond the plant boundary.”

### Anthony callback

Preferred:

> **“This is what shared context buys us: a path from event, to asset, to dependency, to consequence.”**

Alternate:

> **“We’ve moved from locating exposure to understanding dependency.”**

### End frame

Hold on the WPCF, service-area polygon, dependent buildings, and enough hydro context to remember why it matters.

# SCENE 04 — ASK NEXTSPACE: THE ANCHOR QUESTION

**Launcher title:** **Can the system explain why?**  
**Clip target:** 60–90 sec  
**Live chapter target:** ~4:00

This is the intellectual center of the AI portion.

### Before recording

Fix these items:

1. **Source attribution** — the Town of Branford Coastal Resilience Plan must not be attributed to Anchor QEA.
2. **Entity naming** — Ask should use Branford Water Pollution Control Facility.
3. **Evidence labels** — prefer:
   - Documented / regulatory
   - Modeled
   - Connected
   - Expert guidance
   - Inference
   - Unknown / requires validation

### Matt setup

> “You’re going to see this through a conversational interface because it is an easy way to interrogate the system.
>
> But the chat is not the important part.
>
> **The important part is the shared representation of the world underneath it.**”

### Anchor question

> **Using the sources and modeled context available here, what do we know about flood risk to the Branford Water Pollution Control Facility and the wider consequence if service is disrupted? Separate documented evidence, modeled exposure, explicit service relationships, documented mitigation, transferable engineering guidance, and what still requires validation. Cite the source for each.**

### Recording direction

Do not record a person typing the whole prompt.

Preferred:
- have the prompt already entered,
- start recording immediately before Submit,
- click Submit,
- record the full response,
- remove dead generation time in Final Cut.

A tasteful speed ramp is acceptable if needed. Do not fake content.

### Preserve these response behaviors

#### Documented / regulatory evidence
The Branford plan establishes:
- floodplain context,
- FEMA/base-flood-elevation context,
- below-grade vulnerability pathways,
- facility-specific measures such as sealing cracks/conduits and deployable flood doors/hatches.

#### Connected service relationship
The system should connect:
- WPCF,
- sewer network/treatment relationship,
- service geography,
- dependent residences and commercial/industrial areas.

#### Wider consequence as inference
Possible backups, continuity impacts, emergency measures, or permit-management consequences must remain **inference**, not documented historical fact.

#### Transferable AQ guidance
AQ’s Four Shore material can contribute:
- drainage / outfall backflow strategies,
- flood-safe access,
- stormwater improvements,
- potential tide-gate feasibility,
- shoreline measures with explicit limitations.

The system should say these are not Branford prescriptions.

#### What is still unknown
Preserve and, if possible, scroll to:
- equipment elevations,
- flood pathways,
- backup power,
- fuel duration,
- transfer-switch performance,
- storage / redundancy,
- plant access,
- staff ingress / egress,
- incident history,
- site-specific feasibility.

### Matt — spoken extraction

Do not read the answer.

> “This is the part I care about.
>
> It knows this is a wastewater-treatment asset connected to a larger service system.
>
> It can distinguish modeled flood exposure from documented facility vulnerability.
>
> It can bring in engineering guidance without pretending that guidance was written specifically for Branford.
>
> And just as importantly — look at what it says we still don’t know.”

Pause on validation.

> “Equipment elevations. Backup power. Flood pathways. Access. Redundancy.
>
> Those are not details the model should invent.”

Then:

> **“A trustworthy answer isn’t just what the system can tell you. It’s whether it knows where the evidence stops.”**

### Anthony

> “We didn’t make this more trustworthy by giving the model a longer prompt.
>
> **We gave it a better representation of the world it is reasoning about.**”

### Edit direction

This clip can contain hidden cuts:
- during generation,
- between top response and validation section,
- before opening a source.

Use scroll movement to disguise edits.

The audience does not need to read every word. The screen demonstrates depth and provenance; Matt tells them what matters.

# SCENE 07 — TWEED: PAST, PRESENT, PLANNED

**Launcher title:** **What changed over time?**  
**Clip target:** 45–70 sec  
**Live chapter target:** ~2:30

Tweed is a core scene.

Branford demonstrates **dependency + evidence**.

Tweed demonstrates **time + planned future state**.

### Editorial thesis

> **Shared context becomes more valuable when it can represent not only what is here, but what was here, what has happened here, what is planned here, and the evidence behind each version of the place.**

### Shot A — current Tweed

Start on the strongest 3D Tweed airport frame.

Matt:

> “This is Tweed today.”

### Shot B — historical evidence

Cut to one correctly sourced, geolocated historical flood photograph.

If the photo can exist as a geolocated object inside Nextspace, use that. Otherwise, use a clean editorial insert.

Caption:
- source,
- date,
- event,
- location.

Matt:

> “This site also has a history with water.”

### Shot C — recent event evidence

If using recent airport flooding, show one restrained headline or source card.

Matt:

> “And this is not only historical. Recent flooding has disrupted airport operations.”

### Shot D — modeled WGW condition

Return to the twin.

Matt:

> “Now we can place those observations alongside modeled scenarios without pretending they are the same thing.”

### Shot E — planned future

Reveal:
- official future terminal rendering,
- planned airport artifact,
- or a modeled future-state object if available.

Matt:

> “At the same time, this site is changing.”

### Shot F — documented resilience intent

Show one authoritative source card or excerpt describing planned resilience measures.

The point is not to judge the airport. The point is that documented design intent becomes part of the context.

### Anthony

> **“Now our context isn’t just what is here.”**

Pause.

> **“It can connect what happened, what exists, what is planned, and the evidence behind each.”**

### Strong engineering question

> **“What evidence would we need to evaluate whether the planned resilience measures perform as intended under these modeled scenarios?”**

### Final Cut treatment

A slightly more cinematic edit is appropriate:
- match cut current airport → historical photo,
- dissolve historical photo into matching geography,
- cut back into modeled water,
- reveal future rendering.

Keep it elegant and fast. Do not turn it into a documentary montage.

# SCENE 08 — MEDICAL CLINIC BIM: AEC + OPERATE

**Launcher title:** **What is inside the building?**  
**Clip target:** 40–60 sec  
**Live chapter target:** ~1:30–2:00

### Job

Move from:

> **regional exposure**

to:

> **facility continuity**

The BIM matters because context can descend:

> Region → Site → Building → Space → System → Asset → Operational dependency

This directly callbacks Anthony’s presentation:

> Site → Building → Asset → Process

### Recommended BIM additions

Minimum useful additions:

1. **Main electrical switchgear / electrical panel**
2. **Automatic transfer switch**
3. **Emergency generator**, ideally elevated or visibly protected

Optional:
4. generator fuel supply
5. one critical load or critical electrical subsystem

Do not build ten systems. Build **one convincing dependency chain**.

### Target relationship

> Emergency generator  
> → supplies transfer switch  
> → supports critical electrical system  
> → supports clinic operations

And:

> main switchgear / electrical room  
> → is located at a specific elevation / space

### Shot A — regional

Start outside the clinic with relevant flood/exposure context.

Matt:

> “At regional scale, we can identify that a facility may be exposed.”

### Shot B — descend into BIM

Move deliberately site → building → interior system.

Matt:

> “But an owner or operator needs another level of context.”

### Shot C — reveal electrical dependency

Highlight panel/switchgear, transfer switch, generator.

Matt:

> “What is inside the building?
>
> Which systems are critical?
>
> What supports those systems?
>
> And what happens operationally if one of those assets becomes unavailable?”

### Shot D — flood / elevation implication

If the generator is elevated but switchgear is lower, use that contrast.

Do not assert equipment failure unless you have the evidence.

Use:

> “This asset is below the modeled water elevation and warrants engineering review.”

or:

> “This creates a potential continuity dependency that we now know to investigate.”

### Anthony

> **“The BIM gives us geometry. The maintained context gives us consequence.”**

Then:

> “This is how information created through AEC can extend into operate.”

## 17. Final Cut Pro edit blueprint

### Library structure

Recommended Events:
- `00_SELECTS`
- `01_MODEL`
- `02_BRANFORD`
- `03_ASK`
- `04_REUSE`
- `05_TWEED`
- `06_BIM`
- `07_DECIDE`
- `08_OPTIONAL_GEOS`
- `09_EXPORTS`

### Roles

Use roles such as:
- `NEXTSPACE_SCREEN`
- `ARTICLE_INSERT`
- `PHOTO`
- `TITLE`
- `SOURCE_LABEL`
- `MUSIC` — likely unused
- `SFX` — likely unused

Avoid background music during the live demo.

### Compound clips

Create one compound clip per vignette:
- `V01_MODEL_MASTER`
- `V02_BRANFORD_MASTER`
- `V03_ASK_MASTER`
- `V04_REUSE_MASTER`
- `V05_TWEED_MASTER`
- `V06_BIM_MASTER`
- `V07_DECIDE_MASTER`

### Source cards

When showing external sources, use:
- source name,
- document title,
- date,
- short URL/domain,
- one relevant excerpt or headline.

Do not display full browser pages unless the page itself is the point.

### Freeze frames

If a natural final hold is not clean enough, create a Final Cut hold frame and extend it 3–5 seconds in the master. The HTML wrapper can then hold the final video frame indefinitely.

### Transitions

Use mostly:
- hard cuts,
- very short dissolves,
- geographic match cuts.

Avoid:
- page curls,
- dramatic zoom effects,
- presentation-style wipes.

The film craft should make the experience feel **more live**, not less.

## 20. Quality test

Before locking picture, ask of every vignette:

### Story
Can Matt explain the point in one sentence?

### Context
What relationship became visible here that was not visible before?

### Evidence
Can the audience tell what is documented, modeled, inferred, or unknown?

### Restraint
Are we avoiding claims the evidence does not support?

### Reuse
Does this feel like the same connected world rather than another disconnected demo?

### Film
Does the clip start oriented, reveal one meaningful thing, and land on a strong frame?

### Time
If this vignette is skipped, does the broader story still make sense?

If any answer is “no,” simplify before adding more capability.

## 21. Final creative direction

The demo should become progressively more consequential:

> **Where is the water?**  
> **What does it touch?**  
> **What depends on that?**  
> **How do we know?**  
> **What do experts know that the model does not?**  
> **What still requires validation?**  
> **Can the same context survive across tools, time, scale, and decisions?**  
> **What could we change?**

That progression is the film.

The technology supports it.

The final message is not:

> “Look what this digital twin can do.”

It is:

> **“When the model, the evidence, the relationships, the documents, and the operational world share context, people and AI can reason over the same system.”**

And the final callback remains:

> **Ask is one surface. The twin is another. The context underneath is the asset.**
